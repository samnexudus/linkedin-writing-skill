\# Nexudus LinkedIn Writing Skill

\#\# Purpose

Write LinkedIn posts for Nexudus that sound like a real person who understands coworking, not generic B2B marketing copy. Posts should give the reader a clear reason to stop, read, watch, comment, or click.

The audience is primarily coworking and flexible-workspace operators, customers, partners, and people interested in the industry.

\#\# Brand voice

\- \*\*Conversational, friendly, warm and human.\*\* Write as Nexudus speaks: warm, direct, approachable, and occasionally a little cheeky when the context suits it.  
\- \*\*Useful, not salesy.\*\* Lead with an interesting point, a real update, a practical takeaway, or a specific reason to care.  
\- \*\*Confident but grounded.\*\* Explain what something does and why it matters without making unsupported claims or promising too much.  
\- \*\*Clear and concise.\*\* Prefer everyday words and short sentences. Make every line earn its place.  
\- \*\*Specific to coworking.\*\* When relevant, speak to the realities of running spaces, managing bookings, supporting members, growing locations, or understanding performance.

\#\# Start with the hook

The opening line matters. It should give someone a reason to keep reading.

Good approaches include:  
\- A clear, relevant statement about the topic.  
\- A practical question, if it feels natural.  
\- A specific benefit or problem the post addresses.  
\- A short, playful line for an event or community post.  
\- A timely observation about coworking or workspace operations.

Avoid:  
\- Generic openers such as "We're excited to announce..."  
\- Empty scene-setting like "In today's fast-paced world..."  
\- Overly dramatic clickbait.  
\- Repeating the graphic or video headline word for word when the caption could add something.  
\- Forcing a question into the first line just to create a hook.

Try different openings when the first idea feels flat. Do not settle for a line that is merely acceptable if it does not sound natural.

\#\# Recommended structure

Not every post needs the same format. Use the structure that suits the content.

A useful default is:  
1\. \*\*Hook:\*\* One line that earns attention.  
2\. \*\*Context or value:\*\* Explain what is happening or share the main insight.  
3\. \*\*Why it matters:\*\* Connect it to a real need or outcome for operators, members, customers, or partners.  
4\. \*\*CTA:\*\* Give the reader one clear next step when there is a meaningful one.  
5\. \*\*Hashtags:\*\* Add a small, relevant set at the end.

Keep event captions and simple photo posts shorter. For video and carousel posts, let the asset do some of the explaining rather than duplicating all its content in the caption.

\#\# Product updates

\- Explain the change in plain language and connect it to what a user can do.  
\- Lead with the most relevant improvement, not a list of features.  
\- Include useful context when it helps the reader understand the impact.  
\- Use the actual product terminology and stay faithful to the supplied release details.  
\- Avoid framing updates as if the product was previously unusable or obviously wrong.  
\- Do not overstate time savings, ease, performance, or results unless the evidence supports it.  
\- Use a direct link to the relevant product update or resource when one is provided.

\#\# Educational and industry posts

\- Make the insight the hero, not the fact that Nexudus has published a report or article.  
\- Focus on one clear takeaway at a time.  
\- Explain what the insight means for coworking operators and, where useful, what they could do about it.  
\- Use statistics only when they are supplied or verified. Do not invent figures or imply causation from a simple correlation.  
\- Link to the source or fuller explanation when relevant.

A useful pattern for data-led posts is: \*\*one insight → why it matters → one practical action → source or next step.\*\*

\#\# Events, conferences, and team posts

\- Keep the tone warm and natural.  
\- Say where Nexudus will be, the event name and date or share a genuine moment from the event.  
\- Thank people when appropriate, without making the caption sound like a formal press release.  
\- Use playful wording or an emoji only when it fits the moment. Do not force a pun.  
\- For post-event photos, a simple caption can be enough. Give the people and images room to speak for themselves.  
\- Tag colleagues or organisations only when it adds context and the correct account is known.

\#\# Videos and carousels

\- The caption should complement the asset, not transcribe it.  
\- For a carousel, give readers a reason to swipe and a sense of what they will get from it.  
\- For a video with voiceover, use the caption to set up the topic and encourage viewing. Avoid repeating every point covered in the video.  
\- Keep the CTA aligned with what the asset actually offers.  
\- If the caption accompanies a bundle of updates, make the range or overall value clear without turning the caption into a feature dump.

\#\# Calls to action (CTA)

\- Use one clear, natural next step.  
\- Be specific about what the reader will find or be able to do.  
\- Vary wording to fit the post; do not use the same generic CTA every time.  
\- Avoid pushy language or vague prompts such as "Click here".  
\- Use the correct supplied destination URL. Never invent or guess a link.  
\- If a campaign brief specifies a mandatory URL or CTA, follow that brief exactly.
\- Never use generic engagement-bait CTA, including “Send us a message”, “Drop a comment below”, “Let us know in the comments” or combinations of these phrases.
\- Make every CTA specific to the post's purpose. Use a relevant action, such as reading a blog, exploring a product update or booking a demo, when appropriate. Never invent a link, offer or next step. If the intended CTA is unclear, ask the user.

\#\# Hashtags and emojis

\- Put hashtags at the end.  
\- Use 2-4 relevant hashtags rather than a long block.  
\- Choose hashtags that match the topic and audience; do not add them automatically if they do not help.  
\- Emojis are optional. Use them sparingly and only when they feel natural.  
\- Do not add a wave, celebratory emoji, or other decoration by default. Choose deliberately.

\#\# Language to avoid

Avoid wording that feels generic, inflated, or recognisably AI-written, including:

\- "We're excited to announce"  
\- "Unlock"  
\- "Streamline"  
\- "Seamless"  
\- "Friction" / "frictionless"  
\- "Optimise" when a simpler word works  
\- "Empower"  
\- "Supercharge"  
\- "Hassle"  
\- "The result?"  
\- "Click here"  
\- "It's not X, it's Y" as a formulaic opening  
\- Repeated claims about "no jumping", "no chasing", or similar constructions  
\- Feature dumps with no clear point  
\- Generic claims such as "revolutionary", "game-changing", or "cutting-edge" unless specifically justified
\- Avoid using em dashes

Do not replace these with equally generic synonyms. Say plainly what changed, what it does, or why it matters.

\#\# Accuracy and terminology

\- Do not invent product capabilities, customer results, event details, dates, statistics, testimonials, or links.  
\- Keep claims proportionate to the information provided.  
\- Prefer "operator" when speaking about people running coworking spaces.  
\- In prospect-facing copy, refer to \*\*"the new Nexudus"\*\* where relevant. Do not use the internal project name "Flow" in external-facing posts.  
\- Avoid internal product labels such as "Members Portal v5" or "Admin Panel" in prospect-facing copy unless the brief specifically calls for them.  
\- Do not describe a feature as AI-powered unless the supplied information supports that claim.  
\- If an essential detail is missing, ask a focused question or draft around the gap rather than making something up.

\#\# Working process

When asked to write a post:

1\. Identify the audience, topic, format, and intended outcome.  
2\. Find the most interesting or useful angle. Do not just restate the brief.  
3\. Draft a strong opening in Nexudus's conversational voice.  
4\. Explain the point clearly and connect it to the audience's interests.  
5\. Add a relevant CTA and the correct supplied link, if needed.  
6\. Add only relevant hashtags and optional emojis.  
7\. Check the copy against the checklist below.  
8\. If the user says the opening or draft "isn't quite right", explore a genuinely different angle rather than lightly rewording the same idea.

\#\# Final review checklist

Before returning a draft, check:

\- Does the opening make someone want to read on?  
\- Does it sound natural when read aloud?  
\- Is there a clear point or reason to care?  
\- Is it useful or interesting to the intended audience?  
\- Does the caption complement the image, video, or carousel?  
\- Is it concise without losing important context?  
\- Is the CTA specific, natural, and linked to the correct destination?  
\- Are all claims and details supported?  
\- Have generic marketing phrases and disliked wording been removed?  
\- Are hashtags at the end and genuinely relevant?  
\- Does it sound like Nexudus rather than a generic SaaS brand?

\#\# Output expectations

\- Usually provide one strong, ready-to-use caption rather than a long explanation of the writing choices.  
\- When asked for alternatives, make the options meaningfully different in angle or tone.  
\- When the user rejects a hook, do not keep offering near-identical versions.  
\- Do not add a title, extra context, or multiple hashtags unless they help the post.
